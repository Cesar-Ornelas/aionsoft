# Customer Delay on Retainer Workflow

> The signed agreement controls billing, rollover, expiration, suspension, and termination. Do not apply new terms retroactively. Have contract language reviewed by legal counsel.

## 1. Confirm the Retainer Model

Identify what the customer purchased before discussing billing or credits.

| Retainer model | Normal treatment during a customer delay |
| --- | --- |
| Reserved capacity or access | Continue billing while the agreed capacity remains reserved |
| Prepaid hours | Apply the agreement's rollover or expiration rules |
| Fixed deliverables paid monthly | Pause affected milestones and revise the delivery schedule |

If the agreement is unclear, escalate internally before making a commitment to the customer.

## 2. Document the Dependency

Record the following as soon as the dependency is identified:

- Work or feature that is ready
- Customer action, approval, access, content, data, feedback, or testing required
- Customer owner responsible for the action
- Date requested and date needed
- Work and milestones affected
- Proposed testing or review dates
- Follow-up attempts and responses

Use the [Project Dependency & Schedule Impact Notice](../templates/customer-reports/project-dependency-and-schedule-impact-notice.md) for customer-facing documentation.

## 3. Notify the Customer

Send the dependency notice promptly. The message should:

- State that the work is ready for customer action
- Identify the action and requested completion date
- Explain which work is paused or at risk
- Clarify that the original delivery date may no longer apply
- Explain the retainer treatment supported by the agreement
- Request acknowledgement or corrections to the recorded timeline

Keep the language factual and neutral. Do not assign blame or imply that non-billable hours are an outstanding invoice.

## 4. Follow Up and Escalate

Use the agreement's deadlines when they exist. Otherwise, use the following as an internal starting point and adjust it to the project:

| Timing | Action |
| --- | --- |
| Day 0 | Send the dependency notice and requested action date |
| After 3 business days | Send a concise reminder and offer scheduling options |
| After 5 business days | Notify customer and internal project owner of schedule risk |
| After 10 business days | Consider a formal project pause and release of reserved capacity |

Do not promise that work can restart immediately after a long pause.

## 5. Handle Billing and Capacity

- Continue billing only as permitted by the signed agreement.
- Keep reserved capacity available until the customer is notified that it will be released.
- Record billable, included, and additional-investment hours separately.
- Do not silently create rollover balances, discounts, or credits.
- Document any commercial exception, its expiration date, and who approved it.
- Use the [Project Activity & Value Report](../templates/customer-reports/project-activity-and-value-report.md) to show total effort invested.

## 6. Pause the Project

When the delay reaches the applicable threshold:

1. Obtain internal approval to pause the project.
2. Send written notice with the effective pause date.
3. Identify the unresolved customer dependency.
4. State what happens to billing and unused hours under the agreement.
5. Release reserved capacity when permitted.
6. Explain that restart dates depend on team availability.
7. Preserve the project timeline, notices, and supporting communications.

## 7. Restart and Rebaseline

After the customer resolves the dependency:

1. Confirm that all required inputs are complete.
2. Review current team capacity.
3. Assess whether prior work needs revalidation or updates.
4. Provide revised testing and milestone dates.
5. Obtain written acknowledgement of the revised schedule.
6. Update the dependency notice with the resolution dates.
